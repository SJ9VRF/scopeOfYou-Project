Scope of You · Aura Yavary

Why personalization needs a contract.

A personal AI should learn that you prefer concise answers. But that does not mean it should shorten a safety-critical explanation, agree with an incorrect belief, or silently turn a style preference into permission to act.

The core idea: a preference is not a global instruction. It has a scope.

From preferences to contracts

Scope of You models three explicit obligations. APPLY means the preference should affect behavior. SUPPRESS means the model may know the preference but should not apply it in this context. MUST-NOT-AFFECT means the preference is prohibited from changing protected behavior such as factuality or non-sycophancy.

Why reward optimization is not enough

In the controlled post-training loop, raw reward optimization produced a useful negative result: the learned objective improved while personalized behavior degraded. This is the kind of failure that aggregate reward can hide. The recovery loop therefore evaluates behavior directly, mines threshold violations, generates hard cases, and retrains with explicit anchors.

What PCO changes

Personalization Contract Optimization trains on matched interventions over user state, context, and protected dimensions. The goal is not maximum adaptation. The goal is correctly scoped adaptation.

What the evidence says

On the independently worded ContractBench-Implicit suite, PCO-Robust reaches 0.7912, compared with 0.6866 for BATPO and 0.7135 for SFT. Across five training seeds the mean is 0.7910 +/- 0.0008, and the hierarchical user-to-case bootstrap improvement over BATPO is +0.1046 with a 95% CI of [+0.0865, +0.1224]. The generic SFT baseline remains stronger on PersonalBench, so the result is a selectivity-utility tradeoff rather than a universal win.

What this does not prove

The evidence is controlled and largely synthetic. It does not establish safety for autonomous high-stakes actions, real-user longitudinal robustness, or state of the art on public LLM benchmarks. The release separates those future experiments from the claims already executed.
